What the terms mean
Order point
The level that triggers an order. Set it to cover what sells while you wait for the delivery, plus a little extra. Also called the reorder point, or the min.
Order-up-to level
The level an order brings you back up to. The gap between the two numbers is roughly how much you order each time. Also called the max.
Safety stock
Extra stock held to cover the gap between the forecast and what actually sells. The order point includes it.
In-stock target
How often the shelf should still have stock when the next delivery arrives. A higher target means more safety stock. Also called the service level.
Inventory position
On the shelf, plus on order, minus anything already promised to a customer. This is the number the software compares to the order point.
Seasonal profile
The shape of a normal year, as a multiplier on average sales.
Fill rate
Units sold as a share of units customers asked for.
Planned demand
Demand you know is coming that history would not predict, such as a promotion or one large order.
Committed quantity
Stock on the shelf already promised to open customer orders. Here it ships over the first five days, and nobody else can have it.
Carrying cost
What it costs to hold stock for a year, as a share of its value: the money tied up, the space, insurance, and shrinkage.
Shelf life
How long a unit can sit before it has to be written off. Stock ships oldest first, and the software will not order more than it expects to sell with a tenth of the shelf life to spare.
Customer history
Past sales broken out by who bought. It is the raw material for the forecast.

A simplified model running on made-up sales. A real one would run on your own register or sales history, and send orders the way your suppliers take them.
