    What the terms mean
    Customer history
    Past sales broken out by who bought. It is the raw material for the forecast.
    Seasonal profile
    The shape of a normal year, as a multiplier on average sales.
    Planned demand
    Demand you know is coming that history would not predict, such as a promotion or one large contract order.
    Committed quantity
    Stock on the shelf already promised to open customer orders. Here it ships over the first five days, and nobody else can have it.
    Inventory position
    On hand, plus on order, minus committed. This is the number the planner watches.
    Safety stock
    Extra stock held to cover the gap between the forecast and what actually sells.
    Reorder point (min)
    The level that triggers an order: expected demand during the supplier's lead time, plus safety stock.
    Order-up-to level (max)
    The target an order brings you back up to: expected demand over the lead time and the order cycle, plus safety stock. Also written OUTL, and called the max in a min/max system.

    A simplified model running on made-up sales. A real one would run on your own sales history, your open orders, and your suppliers' actual delivery times.
